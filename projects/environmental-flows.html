Environmental Flows in Texas

Watershed delineation, stream reference classification, and analysis of hydrologic alteration metrics across Texas ecoregions.

Project GitHub RepoRelated publication
01Project summaryBaylor research

At the McManamay Lab, I contributed to environmental flows research by automating watershed delineation, modeling reference versus non-reference streams, and examining hydrologic alteration (HIT) metrics associated with fish populations across Texas ecoregions.

02MethodologyAPIs and statistical modeling

I used R and the Model My Watershed API to retrieve watershed boundaries from stream-gage coordinates. I also developed a Random Forest workflow to distinguish reference and non-reference streams using watershed and stream attributes.

For the fish and hydrologic alteration analysis, I compared decision-tree regression fits over increasing tree depths, then averaged R² values across available HIT metrics within each ecoregion. I used numerical derivatives to identify candidate inflection points in the mean model-fit curves. These were in-sample model behavior indicators, not confirmed biological thresholds.

03FiguresOriginal research plots
04Interpretation and limitationsModel behavior

The mean curves made it easier to compare model behavior across regions. Because metrics could stop contributing at different tree depths and the reported R² values were from training data, the candidate inflection points should not be interpreted as validated ecological breakpoints. The research repository documents these caveats and the available code.
