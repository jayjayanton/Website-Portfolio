The problem

What needed to be understood or built?

Development in Quito’s eastern valleys creates a need to examine where built-up land is expanding and how surrounding landscapes change. A useful analysis requires comparable observations over time before differences in imagery can be interpreted as urban change.

My contribution

The work I was responsible for.

I developed acquisition and processing workflows, including temporal benchmarking, a Copernicus Data Space S3 fallback, tiled extraction, spatial mosaicking, tests and sample composites. I am defining the next analytical stages around consistent grids, candidate labels, segmentation experiments and independent error analysis.

Approach

How I worked through the problem.

01

Acquire repeated satellite observations

Retrieve Sentinel-2 L2A imagery through Sentinel Hub or Copernicus Data Space S3. Organize extraction around repeatable periods and a defined area of interest, with fallback acquisition supporting continuity when the preferred route is unavailable.

02

Prepare comparable imagery

Screen unsuitable observations, construct temporal composites and align tiles before mosaicking. Compare annual, quarterly and monthly extraction approaches to understand the tradeoff between available observations and temporal detail.

03

Develop candidate built-up mapping

Planned analysis will derive spectral indices such as NDVI and NDBI, assess candidate reference labels including the ESA WorldCover built-up class, and investigate whether PyTorch CNN segmentation improves built-up mapping.

04

Separate visual change from measured change

The validation plan uses independent reference samples, spatial and temporal holdouts, and error analysis. Only after that stage can built-up masks and change summaries support defensible estimates of urban expansion.

Result & scope

What works now, and what comes next.

The repository contains a working data-preparation foundation and actual annual-composite previews for the Tumbaco pilot. These establish the inputs for the next research stage; segmentation accuracy and urban expansion rates have not yet been established.

Scope & next steps

  • The displayed pilot is near Tumbaco and is not a validated map of all Quito or its valleys.
  • The 2016 and 2025 images are illustrative comparisons; cloud, seasonal and acquisition effects still require validation.
  • Computer vision segmentation, independent accuracy assessment and quantified expansion rates remain planned research outputs.
  • Sentinel-2 imagery supports landscape analysis rather than cadastral surveying, architectural measurement or construction approval.

Learning & progression

How this work develops my practice.

Temporal consistency is a research requirement, not just a preprocessing convenience. Cloud cover, seasonality, observation density and grid alignment can create apparent differences that a change model might otherwise mistake for urban growth.
