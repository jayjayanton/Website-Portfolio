About Jay

Understand the problem.
Build a useful solution.

I connect data science, geospatial analysis, data engineering and software development. I work from the question and the available evidence to build methods and interfaces that can be inspected, used and extended.

Methods that carry across projects.

My projects share a practical sequence: understand the question, reconcile the data, choose the method and make the result usable. That applies when I am comparing models, preparing spatial datasets, integrating APIs or building an interface.

My background includes remote sensing and environmental analysis with NASA DEVELOP, WWF and the U.S. Geological Survey; research and data science at Baylor University; and environmental and energy analytics at NETL through Leidos.

Across these projects, my responsibilities have grown from preparing and interpreting spatial datasets to developing predictive models, building repeatable data pipelines and creating interactive software. I am now the creator and sole developer of PredioAI, which connects architectural plans, property information and geospatial tools.

The case studies show how I extend and combine methods as requirements change. They connect research experience with data systems and applications, and make my contribution, implementation choices and remaining questions visible.

Working approach

How I approach a new problem.

These are recurring practices across the projects in this portfolio.

Frame the question.

Identify the intended use, available data, units and assumptions before choosing a method.

Build a traceable workflow.

Make transformations, model inputs and outputs explicit so that the work can be checked and repeated.

Evaluate the right claim.

Separate exploratory fits from validation results, and document where the data or method limits an interpretation.

Make the result usable.

Choose a map, report, exported dataset or interface that helps the next person understand and use the result.
