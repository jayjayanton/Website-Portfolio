Jay Antonio Oliver

Environmental Data Scientist

Quito, Ecuadorjayjayoliver@hotmail.com+1 (540) 406-9434LinkedInPortfolio & case studies

Profile

Environmental data scientist with 6+ years of experience in remote sensing, land-use analysis, water resources and energy research across NASA DEVELOP, WWF, USGS, Baylor University and Leidos/NETL. Builds reproducible Python and R workflows that connect satellite observations, partner-supplied ground observations and regulatory records with spatial analysis and interpretable models. First author and coauthor of published research, with experience communicating methods, uncertainty and findings through maps, datasets and technical reports.

Core skills

Environmental and spatial analysis: Land-use and land-cover change, watershed delineation, hydrologic alteration, air-quality analysis, spatial classification and greenhouse-gas reporting datasets.

Remote sensing and GIS: ArcGIS Pro, QGIS, GDAL, Rasterio, GeoPandas; Landsat, Sentinel-2, MODIS, VIIRS and CALIOP; raster/vector integration and time-series analysis.

Reproducible research: Python, R, SQL, REST APIs, Random Forest, XGBoost, logistic regression, cross-validation, SHAP, Git, technical reporting and bilingual communication.

Relevant experience

Leidos — Geospatial Data Scientist

Morgantown, WV · NETL research | Feb 2024 – May 2026

  • Developed Random Forest and XGBoost models to screen for likely orphaned wells in a team-assembled inventory exceeding 7 million records, combining spatial, temporal and well-history predictors with stratified validation and SHAP interpretation.
  • Standardized EPA greenhouse-gas reporting data for 2010–2024 into reusable facility-level datasets and built Python/SQL pipelines for Texas wellbore and production records.
  • Converted more than 400 geologic rasters to standardized polygon datasets and prepared basin-specific web maps for NETL atlas publication using ArcGIS Enterprise and Experience Builder.
  • Modeled ten coal-ash oxide constituents with multi-output XGBoost, comparing separate-estimator and shared-tree approaches, log-ratio transformations, grouped validation and constituent-level error diagnostics.

Baylor University — Lab Manager / Geospatial Data Scientist

Waco, TX | Apr 2023 – Feb 2024

  • Served as primary Python developer for 30-meter U.S. land-use and land-cover basemaps covering five observation years from 2008 to 2019; published the research as first author in Scientific Data.
  • Harmonized NLCD, USDA Cropland Data Layer and irrigation data; implemented GCAM, CLM and LUH2 crosswalks and assessed fractional land-cover differences with spatial mean squared error.

Baylor University — Geospatial Data Specialist

Waco, TX | Oct 2021 – Apr 2023

  • Automated watershed delineation in R and developed Random Forest workflows for reference-stream identification, contributing spatial preparation methods to published environmental-flow research.
  • Evaluated decision-tree regressions of hydrologic alteration and fish response across tree depths and regional aggregations, examining model behavior in exploratory ecological analysis.

U.S. Geological Survey — Spatial Analyst

Remote | Jan 2021 – Jul 2021

  • Produced pixel-level land-cover change maps from more than 20 years of Landsat imagery, using R/Python raster workflows, tasseled-cap indices and transition matrices.

World Wildlife Fund — Data Scientist / Statistical Analyst

Remote / Washington, DC | Jan 2020 – Dec 2020

  • Built spatial predictors from Hansen forest-cover and environmental data for 2000–2017; compared logistic models linking forest loss with infrastructure, land use and socioeconomic conditions.

NASA DEVELOP — Remote Sensing Data Scientist

Hampton, VA / Remote | Sep 2019 – Apr 2020

  • Processed more than 900 multidimensional datasets across Central America air-quality and Peru water-resource projects using Python, Linux and GDAL, preparing observations for ArcGIS Pro and statistical analysis.
  • Compared MODIS/VIIRS aerosol optical depth with partner-supplied San Salvador PM2.5 observations, and analyzed NDVI, precipitation, evapotranspiration and soil moisture to investigate mesquite forest decline in Lambayeque.
